Enigma: Cipher Machine

A working Enigma for your iPhone. It started out as the Army’s Enigma I, and now the Navy’s M3 and the U-boat M4 are in there too.

The rotors, reflectors and plugboard are wired like the real machines and step like them, double step included. Set it up the same as any other accurate Enigma and you get the same ciphertext. Go back to the same start position, type the ciphertext in, and your message comes back out.

The keyboard#

This is my favourite part. Switch to the Enigma keyboard in Messages, Mail or anywhere else you type, and every letter comes out enciphered. The app you’re typing in only gets the ciphertext, so what you actually wrote prints on a strip of paper tape above the keys. The reset key takes the rotors back to the start position, for a new message or for reading one you got.

Key sheets#

Whoever you write to needs the same key to read you. A key sheet has a month of daily keys, one row per day: rotor order, ring settings, start position and plugboard pairs. Tap today’s row and the machine is set. Send the sheet to a friend by AirDrop or straight to a phone nearby, and you’re both on the same key.

The machines#

  • Enigma I, Heer and Luftwaffe: three rotors out of five, reflector A, B or C. Comes with the app.

  • Enigma M3, Kriegsmarine: three rotors out of eight. In-app purchase.

  • Enigma M4, the U-boats: a thin fourth rotor next to the reflector, from February 1942. In-app purchase.

Each one has its own box on the shelf and keeps its own settings, key sheets and tape.

Also#

  • Encipher text you’ve already written from the share sheet

  • Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets with today’s key

  • Shortcuts and Siri actions for enciphering and deciphering

Privacy#

Enigma doesn’t collect anything. No analytics, no tracking, no advertising identifier, no account.

Nothing you type goes anywhere. Messages are enciphered on your device and stay there. Key sheets are made on your device too, and when you share one it goes straight to the device you choose, by AirDrop or the share sheet. It never passes through a server of mine, because I don’t have one.

The M3 and M4 are bought through the App Store. Apple handles the payment, and I never see who you are or how you paid.

The keyboard has no network access and doesn’t ask for “full access”.
