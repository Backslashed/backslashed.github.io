Binary O'Clock

There are only 10 types of people in this world: those who understand binary, and those who don’t.

Binary O’Clock turns your screen into a wall of glowing blue lights that tells the time (hours, minutes and seconds) in binary. I made the first version for the Apple TV back in 2016, and it’s been quietly ticking away ever since. Version 2.0 was a complete rewrite in SwiftUI that looks exactly the way it always has. Version 3.0 brings it to the Mac, iPad, iPhone and Apple Watch.

Download on the App Store

Reading it#

Each column is one digit of the time: HH MM SS. Read a column from the bottom up. The lights are worth 1, 2, 4 and 8, so add up the ones that are lit and you have the digit. Some columns are shorter because they never need the bigger numbers: the first hour digit never goes above 2, and the first minute and second digits never go above 5.

The clock above says 10:48:36:

H H M M S S
8 ○ ● ○
4 ○ ● ○ ○ ●
2 ○ ○ ○ ○ ● ●
1 ● ○ ○ ○ ● ○
= 1 0 4 8 3 6

It’s the same time as on the app icon, and has been since 2016.

What’s here#

  • Apple TV: the full-screen clock, sharp on 4K TVs, keeping the screen saver away for as long as it’s showing

  • iPhone and iPad: the full-screen clock, and widgets for the Home Screen, the Lock Screen and StandBy

  • Mac: a window that goes full screen, and a tiny clock in the menu bar, with or without the window

  • Apple Watch: an app, and complications for your watch face

Pro#

The classic blue clock is free. Binary O’Clock Pro, a one-time purchase, adds the White, Aviation and Dust themes, round lights, a learning mode that shows what each light is worth, 12-hour time and a speaking clock. You can try all of it in Settings before you buy, and one purchase unlocks it on Apple TV, Mac, iPad, iPhone and Apple Watch alike.

Privacy#

Binary O’Clock collects nothing. It has no analytics, tracking, advertising identifier or account.

It doesn’t connect to the internet. All it does is read the time from the device it’s running on.
